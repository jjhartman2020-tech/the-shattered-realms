# Combat Philosophy

Combat should follow a consistent, predictable structure while allowing every encounter to feel unique.

Players should always understand what stage of combat they are currently in, what options are available, and what events are about to occur.

The AI Game Master should follow the same combat flow for every encounter unless a unique mechanic explicitly overrides it.

A consistent combat structure allows the AI to make reliable decisions while giving players confidence that encounters are fair and understandable.

---

# When Combat Begins

Combat begins whenever one or more participants intend to oppose another through hostile action and peaceful resolution is no longer the immediate objective.

Combat may begin through many situations, including:

- A direct attack
- An ambush
- A failed negotiation
- A quest event
- A defensive response
- A surprise encounter
- An attempted arrest
- A scripted encounter

The AI Game Master should only enter combat when normal roleplaying can no longer reasonably resolve the situation.

---

# Entering Combat

Once combat begins, the AI Game Master should complete the following steps in order:

1. Identify every participant.
2. Determine each participant's allies and enemies.
3. Establish the battlefield.
4. Resolve awareness.
5. Resolve surprise, if applicable.
6. Determine initiative.
7. Begin the first round.

This sequence should remain consistent for nearly every combat encounter.

---

# Awareness

Before initiative is determined, every participant's awareness is evaluated.

Awareness determines whether a character is prepared for combat before the first round begins.

Common awareness states include:

- Unaware
- Suspicious
- Alert
- Hidden

Awareness may be influenced by factors such as:

- Stealth
- Noise
- Visibility
- Previous knowledge
- Environmental conditions
- Magical or technological detection

Awareness determines whether surprise is possible but does not directly decide turn order.

---

# Surprise

If one or more participants successfully avoid detection before combat begins, surprise may occur.

Surprised participants may begin combat at a disadvantage according to the campaign's rules.

Not every ambush automatically guarantees surprise.

The AI Game Master should evaluate whether each participant had a reasonable opportunity to detect the threat before combat began.

Surprise affects only the opening moments of combat and should not provide an overwhelming advantage beyond the initial phase of the encounter.

---

# Initiative

Once awareness and surprise have been resolved, initiative determines the order in which participants act during combat.

Initiative should establish a clear and consistent turn order for every combat encounter.

Initiative may be determined using any method appropriate for the campaign, including:

- Dice Rolls
- Attribute Scores
- Fixed Initiative Values
- Speed Statistics
- Ability Effects
- Environmental Factors

The AI Game Master should always determine initiative before the first round begins.

Once established, initiative normally remains unchanged unless a specific rule or ability states otherwise.

---

# Round Structure

Combat is divided into rounds.

A round represents every active participant having an opportunity to act.

The duration of a round is intentionally abstract and may vary depending on the pacing of the campaign.

Every round generally follows this sequence:

1. Start of Round
2. Participants take turns according to initiative.
3. End-of-turn effects are resolved.
4. Remaining round effects are resolved.
5. A new round begins if combat continues.

Rounds continue until combat ends.

---

# Turn Structure

Each participant acts once during their turn.

During a turn, participants may perform actions according to the rules defined in **CoreMechanics.md**.

A typical turn consists of:

1. Start of Turn
2. Resolve start-of-turn effects.
3. Choose available actions.
4. Resolve reactions triggered during the turn.
5. Resolve end-of-turn effects.
6. End Turn.

The exact actions available are defined elsewhere to avoid duplicating combat mechanics.

---

# Actions During a Turn

During a participant's turn, they may perform any valid actions available to them.

Examples include:

- Move
- Attack
- Use an Ability
- Defend
- Use an Item
- Interact with the Environment
- Attempt a Skill
- Help an Ally
- Prepare an Action
- Retreat
- Surrender

The availability of each action is determined by the rules defined throughout the combat system.

Combat Flow defines **when** actions occur, while **CoreMechanics.md** defines **how** they function.

---

# Reactions

Certain events may allow participants to respond outside of their normal turn.

These responses are known as reactions.

Examples include:

- Defensive Maneuvers
- Opportunity Attacks
- Counter Abilities
- Protective Actions
- Environmental Responses

Reactions always occur when their triggering conditions are met.

The specific rules governing reactions are defined within **CoreMechanics.md**.

---

# End of Round

Once every active participant has completed their turn, the current round ends.

Before the next round begins, the AI Game Master should resolve any remaining round-based effects.

These may include:

- Status Effect Durations
- Cooldown Reduction
- Environmental Changes
- Ongoing Damage
- Ongoing Healing
- Battlefield Events
- Timed Objectives

After all end-of-round effects have been resolved, a new round begins if combat has not yet ended.

---

# Ending Combat

Combat ends when active hostilities have concluded and no participants intend to continue fighting.

Combat may end for many reasons, including:

- One side is defeated.
- One side surrenders.
- One side retreats successfully.
- A peaceful resolution is reached.
- The encounter objective is completed.
- An outside event interrupts the battle.
- The AI Game Master determines that combat is no longer necessary.

Combat should end naturally rather than forcing every encounter to continue until one side is eliminated.

---

# Victory

Victory does not always mean defeating every opponent.

Examples of victory include:

- Defeating hostile forces.
- Successfully defending an objective.
- Escorting allies to safety.
- Capturing an important target.
- Preventing an enemy from escaping.
- Completing a mission objective.
- Successfully negotiating an end to the conflict.

The AI Game Master should determine victory based on the encounter's objectives rather than only the number of defeated enemies.

---

# Retreat

Any participant may attempt to retreat if the situation allows.

A successful retreat removes that participant from the encounter.

Retreating may result in consequences such as:

- Abandoning objectives.
- Allowing enemies to escape.
- Losing valuable resources.
- Preserving lives for future encounters.

Enemies should also consider retreat when it is logical based on their intelligence, morale, and objectives.

Retreat is a valid tactical decision and should not automatically be considered a failure.

---

# Surrender

Combatants may choose to surrender instead of continuing to fight.

A surrender may be accepted or rejected depending on the circumstances.

Surrender may lead to:

- Capture
- Negotiation
- Imprisonment
- Interrogation
- Exchange of Prisoners
- Recruitment
- Release

The outcome of a surrender should depend on the personalities, goals, and morality of the participants involved.

---

# Defeat

Defeat occurs when participants are no longer capable of continuing the encounter or have failed the encounter's objective.

Defeat does not necessarily mean death.

Possible outcomes include:

- Being Knocked Unconscious
- Capture
- Escape
- Rescue
- Injury
- Loss of Equipment
- Story Consequences

The consequences of defeat should create new opportunities for storytelling rather than always ending the campaign.

---

# Rewards & Consequences

After combat ends, the AI Game Master should resolve all outcomes of the encounter.

Examples include:

- Experience Awards
- Loot Distribution
- Reputation Changes
- Faction Relationship Changes
- Quest Progress
- Crime Consequences
- Companion Reactions
- World State Changes

Each system should follow its own documentation.

Combat Flow determines **when** these outcomes are resolved, while their mechanics are defined in their respective documents.

---

# Standard Combat Flow

Every combat encounter should generally follow this sequence:

1. Combat Begins
2. Participants Identified
3. Battlefield Established
4. Awareness Evaluated
5. Surprise Resolved
6. Initiative Determined
7. Combat Round Begins
8. Participants Take Turns
9. Reactions Resolve When Triggered
10. End-of-Round Effects Resolve
11. Repeat Rounds Until Combat Ends
12. Determine Victory, Retreat, Surrender, or Defeat
13. Resolve Rewards & Consequences
14. Return to Normal Gameplay

Unique encounters may modify this sequence when required by specific mechanics, but the overall structure should remain consistent whenever possible.

---

# Balance Philosophy

Combat Flow exists to provide consistency rather than complexity.

Every encounter should follow a structure that players can understand while remaining flexible enough to support creative gameplay, unique mechanics, and narrative moments.

The combat sequence should minimize confusion by clearly defining when events occur, allowing both players and the AI Game Master to resolve encounters fairly and consistently.

---

# Future Systems

The combat flow framework is designed to support future expansion without requiring major redesign.

Possible future additions include:

- Simultaneous Turns
- Large-Scale Battles
- Naval Combat
- Vehicle Combat
- Space Combat
- Underwater Combat
- Multi-Stage Encounters
- Environmental Events
- Dynamic Objectives
- Reinforcement Systems

Future mechanics should integrate into the existing combat flow while preserving the consistent sequence of combat established by this document.
