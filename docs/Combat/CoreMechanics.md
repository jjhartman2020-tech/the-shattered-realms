# Core Mechanics Philosophy

Core Mechanics define the fundamental rules that every participant follows during combat.

These mechanics establish what actions can be performed, when they may be performed, and how they are resolved.

They intentionally do **not** redefine systems such as Abilities, Equipment, Status Effects, or Leveling.

Instead, they provide the framework through which those systems operate during combat.

---

# Combat Actions

During their turn, a participant may perform actions available to them.

Common actions include:

- Move
- Attack
- Use an Ability
- Defend
- Use an Item
- Interact with the Environment
- Help an Ally
- Prepare an Action
- Retreat
- Surrender

Additional actions may become available through abilities, equipment, quests, or campaign-specific mechanics.

Every action should have a clearly defined purpose and meaningful tactical value.

---

# Action Economy

Action Economy defines how many actions a participant may perform during their turn.

By default, each participant receives one primary action during their turn.

Some abilities, equipment, or special mechanics may grant additional actions or modify how actions are used.

The exact number of available actions should remain easy for both players and the AI Game Master to track.

No build should consistently gain so many additional actions that it removes meaningful decision-making from combat.

---

# Movement

Movement represents a participant changing position on the battlefield.

Movement may occur before, after, or between other actions when allowed by the campaign's rules.

Movement may include:

- Walking
- Running
- Crawling
- Climbing
- Swimming
- Flying
- Jumping

Environmental conditions may affect movement, including:

- Difficult Terrain
- Obstacles
- Weather
- Hazards
- Status Effects

Movement should encourage tactical positioning rather than simply closing distance as quickly as possible.

The detailed effects of terrain and battlefield features are defined within **Environment.md**.

---

# Attacking

An attack is any deliberate action intended to affect another participant through direct offensive force.

Attacks may originate from many sources, including:

- Weapons
- Abilities
- Environmental Objects
- Unarmed Combat
- Improvised Weapons
- Companion Actions

Every attack should clearly identify:

- The attacker
- The target or targets
- The attack method
- The intended effect

The detailed properties of weapons, abilities, and equipment are defined within their respective documentation.

---

## Valid Targets

Before an attack is resolved, the chosen target must be valid.

Target validity may depend on:

- Range
- Visibility
- Line of Sight
- Battlefield Conditions
- Ability Restrictions
- Equipment Restrictions
- Campaign Rules

The AI Game Master should reject attacks against invalid targets before they are resolved.

---

## Resolving an Attack

Every attack follows the same general sequence:

1. Choose an attack.
2. Verify a valid target.
3. Determine whether the attack succeeds.
4. Resolve the attack's effects.
5. Apply any additional effects.
6. Update the battlefield.

This sequence should remain consistent regardless of the attack's source.

---

## Multiple Targets

Some attacks may affect more than one participant.

Examples include:

- Explosions
- Cones
- Lines
- Chains
- Area Effects

Each affected participant resolves the attack independently unless a specific mechanic states otherwise.

---

# Defending

Defending represents any action intended to reduce or avoid harmful effects.

Defense may be active or passive.

Examples include:

- Armor
- Shields
- Dodging
- Blocking
- Parrying
- Defensive Abilities
- Environmental Cover

Every participant should have meaningful defensive options appropriate to the campaign.

---

## Defensive Decisions

Defense should encourage tactical thinking rather than simply increasing survivability.

Players may choose to:

- Accept an attack.
- Reduce incoming damage.
- Avoid the attack entirely.
- Protect an ally.
- Reposition.
- Use a defensive ability.

The best defensive option should depend on the current situation rather than a universally optimal strategy.

---

## Cover

The battlefield itself may provide protection.

Examples include:

- Walls
- Trees
- Vehicles
- Furniture
- Terrain
- Fortifications
- Natural Obstacles

Cover may reduce the effectiveness of attacks, block line of sight, or create new tactical opportunities.

The exact effects of cover are determined by the campaign's combat rules.

---

## Defensive Balance

Defense should reward planning and positioning without making participants impossible to defeat.

Highly defensive strategies should remain viable, but they should involve meaningful trade-offs such as reduced mobility, fewer offensive opportunities, or increased resource usage.

Combat should encourage a balance between offense and defense rather than allowing either approach to dominate every encounter.

---

# Damage

Damage represents the harmful effects of attacks, abilities, environmental hazards, or other harmful events.

Damage should be clear, consistent, and easy for both players and the AI Game Master to resolve.

Every source of damage should identify:

- Source
- Target
- Damage Type
- Damage Amount
- Additional Effects (if any)

The detailed statistics for weapons, abilities, and equipment are defined within their respective documentation.

---

## Damage Types

Campaigns may define any number of damage types appropriate to their setting.

Examples include:

- Physical
- Fire
- Cold
- Lightning
- Poison
- Psychic
- Radiant
- Necrotic
- Energy
- Acid
- Explosive
- Sonic

Damage types should exist to create meaningful tactical choices rather than unnecessary complexity.

---

## Damage Modifiers

Damage may be increased or reduced by many factors.

Examples include:

- Equipment
- Attributes
- Abilities
- Status Effects
- Environmental Conditions
- Critical Successes
- Defensive Effects

All modifiers should be resolved before applying the final damage.

---

## Resistance, Vulnerability & Immunity

Some participants may naturally resist or be especially vulnerable to certain damage types.

Possible interactions include:

- Resistance
- Vulnerability
- Immunity
- Temporary Protection

These interactions should create strategic variety without making any single damage type universally superior.

The specific values for these interactions are determined by the campaign.

---

## Damage Resolution

Every damaging effect should generally follow this sequence:

1. Determine the source.
2. Confirm the target.
3. Calculate modifiers.
4. Apply defensive effects.
5. Apply final damage.
6. Resolve any additional effects.

Using a consistent resolution order helps prevent conflicts between mechanics.

---

# Resources

Many combat actions consume resources.

Resources limit how often powerful actions may be performed and encourage meaningful decision-making.

Common resources include:

- Health
- Mana
- Stamina
- Energy
- Charges
- Ammunition
- Special Class Resources

Campaigns may introduce additional resources when appropriate.

---

## Resource Costs

Different actions may consume different resources.

Examples include:

- Casting an Ability
- Using Special Equipment
- Activating Defensive Skills
- Performing Powerful Attacks
- Summoning Creatures

Resource costs should encourage players to think strategically rather than simply using their strongest option every turn.

---

## Resource Recovery

Resources may recover through various methods.

Examples include:

- Time
- Rest
- Abilities
- Equipment
- Consumable Items
- Environmental Effects
- Quest Rewards

Different resources may recover at different rates depending on the campaign.

---

## Resource Management

Managing resources is an important part of tactical combat.

Players should balance immediate effectiveness with long-term survival.

Using powerful abilities too early may leave a participant vulnerable later in the encounter, while being overly conservative may allow opportunities to pass.

Resource management should reward planning rather than punish experimentation.

---

## Resource Balance

Resources should provide meaningful limitations without making combat feel restrictive.

Players should regularly make interesting decisions about when to spend or conserve their available resources.

The AI Game Master should manage enemy resources using the same principles whenever practical to ensure fair and believable encounters.

---

# Reactions

Reactions are actions performed in response to a specific triggering event rather than during a participant's normal turn.

Reactions allow combat to remain dynamic by enabling participants to respond immediately to changing battlefield conditions.

A reaction may only occur when its triggering conditions are satisfied.

If no valid trigger occurs, the reaction cannot be used.

---

## Common Reaction Triggers

Examples include:

- An enemy enters range.
- An ally is attacked.
- An attack misses.
- An attack hits.
- A participant moves away.
- An ability is activated.
- An environmental hazard is triggered.
- A status effect is applied.

Campaigns may define additional reaction triggers when appropriate.

---

## Resolving Reactions

When a reaction is triggered, the AI Game Master should:

1. Verify that the trigger is valid.
2. Confirm the participant can perform the reaction.
3. Resolve the reaction.
4. Continue resolving the original event.

Reactions should interrupt combat only long enough to resolve their effects before normal combat resumes.

---

## Reaction Balance

Reactions should provide meaningful tactical opportunities without overwhelming the normal turn structure.

Powerful reactions should require significant investment, careful timing, or meaningful trade-offs.

Combat should remain easy to follow even when multiple reactions occur during the same encounter.

---

# Critical Success & Failure

Some campaigns may include critical outcomes that represent exceptionally successful or exceptionally unsuccessful actions.

Critical results should create memorable moments without making combat feel unfair or excessively random.

Campaigns may choose whether critical outcomes are determined by dice, probability, narrative events, or another system.

---

## Critical Success

A critical success represents an unusually effective result.

Possible effects include:

- Increased Damage
- Improved Healing
- Additional Effects
- Bonus Movement
- Resource Recovery
- Extra Tactical Opportunities
- Enhanced Environmental Interaction

Critical successes should reward good fortune without completely deciding the outcome of an encounter.

---

## Critical Failure

A critical failure represents an unusually poor result.

Possible effects include:

- Missing the Target
- Reduced Effectiveness
- Temporary Disadvantage
- Lost Resources
- Unexpected Positioning
- Environmental Consequences

Critical failures should create interesting situations rather than excessively punishing players.

The goal is to introduce variety and memorable moments, not frustration.

---

## Optional Critical Rules

Campaign creators may customize or disable critical outcomes entirely.

Examples include:

- No Critical System
- Successes Only
- Failures Only
- Expanded Critical Tables
- Narrative Critical Outcomes
- Genre-Specific Critical Rules

The combat framework should support multiple approaches without requiring any single implementation.

---

## Consistency

Critical outcomes should always follow clearly defined rules.

Participants should understand:

- What can critically succeed.
- What can critically fail.
- How critical outcomes are determined.
- What effects they may produce.

Consistency allows both players and the AI Game Master to resolve exceptional situations fairly and predictably.

---

# Standard Combat Turn

While individual campaigns may modify specific mechanics, a participant's turn should generally follow a consistent sequence.

## Turn Sequence

1. Start Turn
2. Resolve Start-of-Turn Effects
3. Assess the Battlefield
4. Choose Available Action(s)
5. Resolve Chosen Action(s)
6. Resolve Triggered Reactions
7. Resolve End-of-Turn Effects
8. End Turn

Maintaining a consistent turn structure allows players and the AI Game Master to understand exactly when events occur during combat.

---

## Decision Making

Every turn should present meaningful choices.

Participants should regularly evaluate:

- Current Health
- Available Resources
- Positioning
- Nearby Allies
- Nearby Enemies
- Active Status Effects
- Remaining Objectives
- Environmental Hazards

Combat should reward thoughtful decisions rather than repetitive strategies.

---

## Battlefield Awareness

The battlefield should constantly evolve throughout an encounter.

Participants should consider:

- Enemy Positions
- Ally Positions
- Cover
- Terrain
- Hazards
- Objectives
- Line of Sight
- Remaining Resources

No single tactic should remain optimal in every situation.

---

## Combat Objectives

Defeating opponents is only one possible objective.

Combat encounters may instead focus on:

- Defending a Location
- Escorting Allies
- Capturing a Target
- Escaping Danger
- Activating Objectives
- Destroying Structures
- Delaying Enemies
- Surviving for a Period of Time

Objectives should encourage varied encounter design and reduce repetitive combat.

---

# Balance Philosophy

Combat should reward intelligent decision-making, teamwork, preparation, and creativity.

Victory should rarely depend on a single statistic, overpowered ability, or repetitive strategy.

Every combat option should have situations where it is valuable and situations where it is less effective.

The AI Game Master should encourage adaptation by presenting varied enemies, environments, and objectives.

Mechanics should remain easy to understand individually while creating meaningful depth through their interaction.

Combat should feel fair, consistent, and rewarding regardless of campaign setting.

---

# Future Systems

The combat framework is designed to support future expansion without requiring significant redesign.

Possible future additions include:

- Mounted Combat
- Naval Combat
- Vehicle Combat
- Space Combat
- Underwater Combat
- Large-Scale Warfare
- Siege Combat
- Interactive Battlefield Events
- Dynamic Weather Effects
- Cooperative Tactical Maneuvers
- Advanced AI Behaviors
- Campaign-Specific Combat Rules

Future mechanics should integrate naturally into the existing combat framework while preserving consistency and player freedom.
