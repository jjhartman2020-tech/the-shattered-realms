# Enemy AI Philosophy

Enemy behavior should create encounters that feel intelligent, believable, and tactically engaging.

Enemies should make decisions based on the information available to them rather than possessing perfect knowledge of the battlefield.

Well-designed AI should challenge players by making logical decisions instead of relying solely on increased statistics.

Different enemies should feel distinct through their behavior, priorities, and tactics.

---

# AI Decision Making

Every enemy should evaluate the battlefield before acting.

When determining an action, the AI Game Master should consider factors such as:

- Current Health
- Available Resources
- Positioning
- Nearby Allies
- Nearby Enemies
- Active Status Effects
- Current Objectives
- Environmental Hazards
- Known Threats

The importance of each factor may vary depending on the enemy's role, personality, and current situation.

---

## Decision Priorities

When multiple valid actions are available, the AI should generally prioritize actions that best support its immediate objective.

Examples include:

- Eliminating a dangerous opponent.
- Protecting an ally.
- Securing an objective.
- Escaping danger.
- Holding a defensive position.
- Delaying opponents.
- Supporting stronger allies.

The AI should choose actions that are consistent with its goals rather than selecting actions randomly.

---

# Combat Roles

Different enemies should behave according to their intended role within an encounter.

Common combat roles include:

- Frontline Fighters
- Ranged Attackers
- Support Units
- Healers
- Assassins
- Tanks
- Summoners
- Controllers
- Leaders

Combat roles should influence decision-making without making behavior completely predictable.

---

## Role Identity

Enemies with the same statistics may still feel completely different because of how they behave.

For example:

A frontline fighter may aggressively pressure opponents.

A healer may avoid direct combat while supporting allies.

An assassin may prioritize isolated targets before retreating.

A leader may coordinate nearby allies instead of maximizing personal damage.

Behavior should reinforce an enemy's identity as much as its statistics do.

---

## Consistency

Enemy decisions should remain understandable and internally consistent.

Participants should be able to recognize patterns over time without every encounter becoming predictable.

Consistent behavior allows players to develop strategies while still rewarding adaptation when battlefield conditions change.

---

# Tactical Decision Making

Enemy actions should be guided by tactical reasoning rather than randomness.

Each turn, the AI Game Master should evaluate the battlefield and determine which available action best supports the enemy's current objective.

Factors that may influence tactical decisions include:

- Current Health
- Available Resources
- Positioning
- Distance to Targets
- Active Status Effects
- Nearby Allies
- Nearby Enemies
- Battlefield Hazards
- Mission Objectives

Enemy decisions should remain logical, even when those decisions are not optimal.

---

## Offensive Decisions

When acting offensively, enemies should evaluate the most effective way to pressure opponents.

Examples include:

- Attacking exposed targets.
- Using powerful abilities at key moments.
- Controlling important areas.
- Preventing enemy movement.
- Interrupting dangerous actions.
- Applying status effects.

Enemies should avoid repeating the same action every turn when better opportunities exist.

---

## Defensive Decisions

Enemies should recognize when survival is more valuable than aggression.

Examples include:

- Retreating to safety.
- Seeking cover.
- Protecting important allies.
- Using defensive abilities.
- Conserving limited resources.
- Waiting for reinforcements.

Choosing to defend should be viewed as a tactical decision rather than a failure to attack.

---

## Objective-Based Decisions

Not every encounter is won by defeating opponents.

When objectives exist, enemies should prioritize actions that support those objectives.

Examples include:

- Guarding an entrance.
- Escorting an important character.
- Activating or defending an objective.
- Delaying opponents.
- Preventing escape.
- Capturing a location.

An enemy's behavior should reflect its purpose within the encounter.

---

# Target Selection

Choosing the correct target is one of the most important decisions an enemy can make.

Target selection should be based on battlefield conditions rather than random choice.

The AI Game Master should evaluate multiple factors before determining which participant to engage.

---

## Target Priorities

Possible considerations include:

- Immediate Threat Level
- Distance
- Accessibility
- Current Health
- Defensive Position
- Active Status Effects
- Strategic Importance
- Mission Objectives

No single factor should always determine the chosen target.

---

## Threat Assessment

Enemies should naturally recognize participants who represent greater danger.

Threat may be created through actions such as:

- Inflicting significant damage.
- Healing allies.
- Protecting objectives.
- Controlling the battlefield.
- Disrupting enemy plans.
- Eliminating key allies.

Threat should be earned through actions rather than automatically assigned.

---

## Changing Targets

Enemies should not remain permanently focused on one opponent.

Changing targets may become appropriate when:

- A higher-priority threat appears.
- The current target becomes unreachable.
- An objective changes.
- Battlefield conditions shift.
- An ally requires immediate assistance.
- A better tactical opportunity develops.

Changing targets should feel deliberate rather than unpredictable.

---

## Avoiding Perfect Knowledge

Enemies should only act on information they could reasonably possess.

They should not automatically know:

- Hidden participants.
- Future player decisions.
- Secret objectives.
- Unknown abilities.
- Information gained outside the encounter.

The AI Game Master should distinguish between what the players know and what the enemies know.

Believable limitations create more immersive and fair encounters.

---

# Group Coordination

Enemies should behave like members of an organized force rather than isolated individuals whenever appropriate.

Coordinated behavior creates more engaging encounters while reinforcing the identity of enemy factions and organizations.

The level of coordination should depend on the intelligence, training, communication, and leadership of the group.

---

## Coordinated Actions

Enemies may cooperate to accomplish shared objectives.

Examples include:

- Focusing attacks on a dangerous opponent.
- Defending vulnerable allies.
- Controlling important areas.
- Holding defensive formations.
- Creating flanking opportunities.
- Supporting retreating allies.
- Protecting key objectives.

Cooperation should emerge naturally from the group's goals rather than from perfect coordination.

---

## Leadership

Some enemies naturally guide the actions of others.

Leaders may:

- Issue commands.
- Coordinate attacks.
- Organize defensive positions.
- Rally nearby allies.
- Adapt battlefield strategy.
- Prioritize mission objectives.

Removing or isolating a leader may reduce the effectiveness or coordination of the remaining group, depending on the encounter.

---

## Communication

Enemies should only coordinate using information they could reasonably share.

Communication may occur through:

- Speech
- Gestures
- Signals
- Magical Communication
- Technology
- Preplanned Strategies

Poor communication, disrupted signals, or separated allies should reduce coordination when appropriate.

---

# Adaptive Behavior

Enemies should respond intelligently as the battlefield changes.

Behavior should evolve based on new information rather than following a fixed sequence of actions.

Adaptation creates encounters that feel responsive while rewarding player creativity.

---

## Responding to Battlefield Changes

Enemies may alter their strategy when:

- An ally is defeated.
- A leader falls.
- Reinforcements arrive.
- A powerful ability is revealed.
- Terrain changes.
- An objective changes.
- Resources become limited.
- A new threat appears.

Changing circumstances should naturally influence future decisions.

---

## Learning During Combat

Enemies may recognize patterns that occur during an encounter.

Examples include:

- Repeatedly using the same ability.
- Frequently targeting the same location.
- Relying on one defensive strategy.
- Protecting a specific ally.
- Avoiding certain areas.

Enemies should adapt to observable patterns without gaining knowledge they have not earned.

---

## Morale

Not every enemy should fight until defeated.

Morale may influence whether enemies choose to:

- Advance.
- Hold Position.
- Retreat.
- Surrender.
- Call for Reinforcements.
- Protect a Leader.
- Abandon an Objective.

Morale may be affected by:

- Heavy casualties.
- Loss of leadership.
- Overwhelming opposition.
- Successful attacks.
- Nearby allies.
- Mission importance.

Morale should reflect the personality and discipline of the enemy rather than following identical rules for every encounter.

---

## Predictability vs. Variety

Enemy behavior should be consistent enough for players to recognize patterns while remaining varied enough to avoid becoming repetitive.

Players should be rewarded for learning enemy tendencies without being able to predict every action with certainty.

The AI Game Master should balance consistency, adaptation, and unpredictability to create encounters that remain engaging over time.

---

# Difficulty Scaling

Increasing difficulty should primarily improve enemy decision-making rather than simply increasing statistics.

Smarter enemies create more engaging encounters than enemies with inflated health or damage values.

Campaigns may adjust difficulty by modifying behavior, resources, encounter composition, or other mechanics appropriate to their setting.

---

## Methods of Scaling

Difficulty may be increased through:

- Improved Tactical Decisions
- Better Positioning
- Stronger Group Coordination
- More Effective Resource Management
- Smarter Ability Usage
- Greater Battlefield Awareness
- More Complex Objectives
- Better Equipment

Statistics may also be adjusted when appropriate, but they should not be the primary method of increasing challenge.

---

## Fairness

Enemy decisions should remain fair and understandable.

The AI Game Master should avoid:

- Using information the enemy could not reasonably know.
- Making impossible or contradictory decisions.
- Ignoring previously established behavior.
- Overriding player agency without justification.

Players should feel defeated because the enemy made good decisions—not because the AI ignored its own rules.

---

## Variety

Different encounters should challenge players in different ways.

Examples include:

- Aggressive opponents.
- Defensive formations.
- Mobile skirmishers.
- Powerful spellcasters.
- Large groups of weaker enemies.
- Small groups of elite enemies.
- Objective-focused encounters.
- Environmental challenges.

Variety encourages players to adapt instead of relying on a single strategy.

---

# Balance Philosophy

Enemy AI exists to create engaging stories and meaningful tactical decisions.

The purpose of intelligent enemies is not to defeat the players at every opportunity, but to create believable opposition that responds naturally to the world around it.

Encounters should reward:

- Preparation
- Teamwork
- Adaptation
- Creative Thinking
- Tactical Positioning
- Resource Management

The AI Game Master should seek to create memorable encounters rather than perfect efficiency.

A believable mistake made by an enemy is often more immersive than a mathematically optimal decision.

---

## Player Agency

Enemy intelligence should enhance player decision-making rather than remove it.

Players should always have opportunities to:

- Learn enemy behavior.
- Counter enemy strategies.
- Exploit mistakes.
- Create advantages.
- Influence the battlefield.
- Achieve objectives through multiple approaches.

Combat should reward intelligent play from both sides.

---

# Future Systems

The enemy AI framework is designed to support future expansion without changing its core principles.

Possible future additions include:

- Personality Profiles
- Fear and Courage Systems
- Diplomacy During Combat
- Reinforcement Systems
- Dynamic Faction Relationships
- Civilian Behavior
- Wildlife Behavior
- Siege AI
- Naval AI
- Vehicle AI
- Large-Scale Army Command
- Campaign-Specific AI Modules

Future systems should build upon the existing principles of consistency, fairness, and believable decision-making while allowing campaign creators to develop increasingly complex encounters.
