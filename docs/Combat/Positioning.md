# Positioning Philosophy

Positioning is one of the most important elements of tactical combat.

Where a participant stands should influence the decisions they make, the opportunities available to them, and the risks they face.

Good positioning should reward planning, teamwork, and awareness rather than simply moving as close to the enemy as possible.

The battlefield itself should become an active part of every encounter.

---

# Battlefield Positioning

Every participant occupies a position within the battlefield.

That position influences interactions such as:

- Movement
- Attacking
- Defending
- Protecting Allies
- Avoiding Hazards
- Reaching Objectives
- Escaping Danger

Position should always matter during combat.

Ignoring positioning should rarely be the optimal strategy.

---

# Movement & Positioning

Movement allows participants to improve or change their position.

Movement decisions should involve meaningful trade-offs.

Examples include:

- Advancing toward an enemy.
- Falling back to safety.
- Protecting an ally.
- Reaching cover.
- Escaping hazardous terrain.
- Controlling an objective.

Participants should regularly evaluate whether moving creates a greater advantage than remaining in place.

# Distance

Distance influences many combat interactions.

Examples include:

- Attack Range
- Ability Range
- Defensive Positioning
- Supporting Allies
- Objective Control
- Escape Routes

Campaigns may measure distance using any appropriate method, including:

- Grids
- Hexes
- Zones
- Measured Distance
- Narrative Positioning

The combat framework supports multiple approaches while preserving consistent tactical decision-making.

# Prototype Grid Rules

The first playable prototype uses a **square grid**.

- Every combatant has an `(x, y)` square position.
- Distance is currently measured by orthogonal square steps: horizontal plus vertical distance.
- A combatant cannot end movement on a square occupied by another active combatant.
- A combatant may move no farther in one movement action than their current **Movement** value.
- Movement continues to use the Speed-based formula defined in Stats.md.
- Standard melee attacks have **range 1 square** unless a weapon, ability, size, or effect states otherwise.
- Ranged weapons and abilities may define larger attack ranges.
- An attack against a target outside the attacker's legal range is rejected before any attack roll is made.

These rules are the prototype implementation of the broader positioning framework. Cover, line of sight, flanking, hazards, terrain costs, and more advanced reach rules will layer onto this grid rather than replacing it.

# Cover

Cover represents any object, terrain, or environmental feature that provides protection during combat.

Effective use of cover should improve survivability while encouraging movement and tactical decision-making.

Examples of cover include:

- Walls
- Trees
- Boulders
- Buildings
- Vehicles
- Furniture
- Fortifications
- Natural Terrain

The exact benefits of cover are determined by the campaign.

---

## Using Cover

Participants may use cover to:

- Reduce exposure to attacks.
- Break line of sight.
- Create safer positions.
- Defend objectives.
- Protect allies.
- Prepare future actions.

Choosing when to leave or remain behind cover should be a meaningful tactical decision.

---

## Dynamic Cover

Not all cover remains unchanged throughout combat.

Examples include:

- Destroyed structures
- Moving vehicles
- Falling debris
- Collapsing terrain
- Magical barriers
- Temporary defenses

The battlefield should evolve when appropriate, encouraging participants to continually reassess their positioning.

---

# Flanking

Flanking occurs when multiple participants coordinate their positioning to pressure or surround an opponent.

Flanking should reward teamwork, communication, and battlefield awareness.

Campaigns may define different rules for determining when a participant is considered flanked.

---

## Advantages of Flanking

Potential benefits may include:

- Improved offensive opportunities.
- Reduced defensive options for the target.
- Better positioning for allies.
- Increased tactical pressure.
- Greater control of the battlefield.

The exact mechanical effects are determined by the campaign.

---

## Preventing Flanks

Participants may reduce the effectiveness of flanking by:

- Maintaining strong positioning.
- Staying near allies.
- Using terrain effectively.
- Controlling narrow spaces.
- Repositioning before becoming surrounded.

Good positioning should help prevent dangerous situations before they occur.

---

# Reach & Engagement

Some participants can interact with opponents from greater distances than others.

Reach represents the effective distance from which a participant can influence nearby combat.

Reach may be affected by:

- Weapons
- Abilities
- Equipment
- Size
- Environmental Conditions
- Campaign Rules

---

## Engagement

Engagement occurs when participants are close enough to directly influence one another's actions.

Examples include:

- Melee combat
- Defensive stances
- Blocking movement
- Protecting allies
- Creating zones of control

Campaigns may define specific rules governing engagement.

---

## Controlling Space

Positioning is not only about where a participant stands but also about the space they threaten or protect.

Participants may influence surrounding areas by:

- Guarding entrances.
- Holding defensive positions.
- Blocking escape routes.
- Protecting objectives.
- Restricting enemy movement.

Controlling space should reward strategic planning rather than raw statistics alone.

---

# Line of Sight

Line of Sight determines whether a participant can directly perceive or interact with another participant or location.

Many combat actions require a clear line of sight before they can be performed.

Factors that may affect line of sight include:

- Terrain
- Cover
- Obstacles
- Darkness
- Weather
- Smoke
- Illusions
- Environmental Effects

Campaigns may define additional conditions that influence visibility.

---

## Blocking Line of Sight

Line of sight may be partially or completely blocked.

Examples include:

- Walls
- Buildings
- Dense Vegetation
- Heavy Fog
- Magical Barriers
- Elevation Changes
- Closed Doors

Blocking line of sight should encourage participants to reposition and use the battlefield creatively.

---

## Visibility

Not every participant on the battlefield is equally visible.

Visibility may change due to:

- Distance
- Lighting
- Stealth
- Camouflage
- Weather
- Environmental Hazards
- Magical Effects

Reduced visibility should create uncertainty while remaining fair and consistent.

---

# Battlefield Control

Positioning is about more than individual movement.

Participants constantly shape the battlefield through their actions and positioning.

Battlefield control includes:

- Holding Objectives
- Defending Allies
- Limiting Enemy Movement
- Creating Safe Areas
- Controlling Chokepoints
- Denying Access
- Forcing Repositioning

Successful participants should influence how combat unfolds rather than simply reacting to it.

---

## Terrain Interaction

The battlefield should provide opportunities for meaningful interaction.

Examples include:

- Destroying Structures
- Activating Mechanisms
- Triggering Hazards
- Creating Obstacles
- Using Environmental Objects
- Changing Elevation
- Opening or Closing Pathways

Interactive environments encourage creativity and make encounters feel unique.

---

## Positioning Priorities

Effective positioning often requires balancing multiple priorities.

Participants should regularly consider:

- Personal Safety
- Ally Protection
- Offensive Opportunities
- Defensive Advantages
- Objective Progress
- Resource Conservation
- Environmental Risks

No single factor should always outweigh the others.

---

# Balance Philosophy

Positioning should reward awareness, planning, and teamwork.

A participant with superior positioning should often outperform one with better statistics but poor tactical decisions.

The battlefield should provide multiple viable paths to success rather than encouraging a single optimal strategy.

Movement and positioning should remain meaningful throughout every stage of combat.

---

# Future Systems

The positioning framework is designed to support future expansion without altering its core principles.

Possible future additions include:

- Vertical Combat
- Flying Movement
- Burrowing
- Climbing
- Swimming
- Teleportation
- Portal Mechanics
- Dynamic Terrain
- Environmental Destruction
- Weather-Driven Positioning
- Large-Scale Battlefields
- Campaign-Specific Positioning Rules

Future mechanics should integrate naturally into the existing positioning system while preserving consistency, tactical depth, and player choice.
