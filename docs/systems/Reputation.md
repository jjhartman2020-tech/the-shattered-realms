# Reputation Philosophy

Reputation is one of the core systems that makes the world feel alive. It represents how individuals, groups, and societies perceive a person based on information they have gathered over time.

Reputation is **not** the same as truth.

A person's reputation is shaped by rumors, eyewitnesses, stories, propaganda, lies, accomplishments, failures, and personal experiences. As a result, a reputation may be completely accurate, partially true, or entirely false.

The goal of the reputation system is to create a world where actions have meaningful social consequences without requiring every character to possess perfect knowledge.

---

## Core Philosophy

The reputation system follows one simple principle:

> People react to what they believe, not necessarily to what is true.

This distinction allows the AI to create believable misunderstandings, political manipulation, secret heroes, infamous criminals, and evolving legends without breaking immersion.

---

## The Three Layers of Reality

Every important event in the game exists on three separate layers.

### Truth

What actually happened.

Truth is objective and exists regardless of whether anyone witnessed it.

Examples:

- The player assassinated a corrupt noble.
- A dragon destroyed a village.
- An innocent merchant stole a magical artifact.

Truth never changes.

---

### Memory

What a specific individual personally knows or experienced.

Each NPC has their own memories.

Examples:

- A guard witnessed the assassination.
- A merchant heard screaming but never saw the killer.
- A traveler arrived after everything was over.

No two NPCs are guaranteed to remember the same event in the same way.

Memories may be forgotten, distorted, or intentionally hidden.

---

### Reputation

What the world believes.

Reputation is formed from the spread of information.

Examples:

- The kingdom believes rebels killed the noble.
- The guard knows the player was responsible.
- The public believes the player is a hero.
- Criminal organizations know the truth.

Reputation can change over time as new evidence appears.

---

## Reputation Is Information

Reputation should be treated as information, not as a simple score.

Different people may have different opinions about the exact same person depending on:

- What they have witnessed
- What they have heard
- Who they trust
- Their personal beliefs
- Their faction
- Their culture
- Their morals
- Their previous experiences

This allows multiple reputations to exist simultaneously.

---

## Player Reputation

The player develops a reputation throughout the world.

Different kingdoms, cities, factions, and social groups may all view the player differently.

The player may become:

- A celebrated hero.
- A feared assassin.
- A respected mercenary.
- A dangerous criminal.
- A mysterious traveler.
- A forgotten nobody.

These reputations may exist simultaneously depending on where the player travels.

---

## NPC Reputation

Important NPCs also possess reputations.

Every major NPC has a public image that influences how other characters interact with them.

Examples include:

- Honorable Knight
- Corrupt Noble
- Legendary Blacksmith
- Ruthless Pirate
- Brilliant Scholar
- Holy Saint

These reputations are not guaranteed to reflect reality.

---

## Reputation Creates Story

The purpose of the reputation system is not to reward or punish the player.

Its purpose is to generate believable stories.

The world should naturally react to the player's actions based on what people know, believe, or assume.

This allows reputation to become one of the primary drivers of dynamic storytelling throughout the game.# Truth, Memory, and Reputation

Every significant event in the game exists on three separate layers:

1. Truth
2. Memory
3. Reputation

These systems are completely independent from one another.

Keeping these systems separate allows the world to naturally produce mysteries, misunderstandings, false accusations, political manipulation, secret identities, and legendary stories without requiring scripted events.

---

## Truth

Truth is what actually happened.

Truth is objective.

It exists regardless of whether anyone witnessed the event.

Truth cannot be changed through rumors or public opinion.

Examples:

- The player stole a priceless artifact.
- A corrupt king secretly ordered an assassination.
- A dragon attacked because it was defending its nest.

Truth always remains the same.

The AI uses truth as the foundation for every event.

---

## Memory

Memory represents what an individual personally knows.

Every NPC possesses their own memories.

Memories are built through:

- Personal experiences
- Conversations
- Witnessing events
- Being told information
- Reading books or letters
- Investigations
- Rumors they choose to believe

Because memories are personal, two NPCs may remember the exact same event differently.

Example:

A tavern fight breaks out.

The bartender remembers:

> "The player defended themselves."

A drunk customer remembers:

> "The player started everything."

A guard remembers:

> "By the time I arrived, the fight was already over."

Each memory is individually true from that character's perspective.

---

## Reputation

Reputation is public perception.

Unlike memory, reputation belongs to communities rather than individuals.

Reputation spreads through information.

Sources include:

- Eyewitnesses
- Rumors
- Newspapers
- Bards
- Merchants
- Travelers
- Government announcements
- Religious organizations
- Criminal networks
- Gossip

As information spreads, it changes.

Stories become exaggerated.

Details disappear.

Facts become distorted.

Eventually the world's version of an event may differ greatly from reality.

---

## How Information Travels

Information should not instantly spread across the world.

Instead, it spreads naturally.

Factors include:

- Number of witnesses
- Credibility of witnesses
- Distance
- Trade routes
- Cities
- Kingdom borders
- Messenger services
- Magical communication
- Political censorship

Major world events travel much faster than local incidents.

A village robbery may never leave the village.

The assassination of a king may become known across the continent.

---

## Lies and Propaganda

Because Truth, Memory, and Reputation are separate systems, information can be manipulated.

Characters may intentionally:

- Lie
- Spread rumors
- Hide evidence
- Bribe witnesses
- Forge documents
- Create fake heroes
- Frame innocent people

These actions alter reputation without changing the truth.

---

## Unknown Truth

Many events should remain unresolved from the player's perspective.

The player is not entitled to know the truth.

If every mystery were immediately explained, the world would lose much of its depth.

Instead, players should investigate events themselves.

Different NPCs may provide:

- Conflicting accounts
- Partial truths
- Honest mistakes
- Deliberate lies

The player must decide who to trust.

---

## Design Principles

The AI should always follow these rules:

- Truth never changes unless reality itself changes.
- Memories belong to individuals.
- Reputation belongs to societies.
- Information spreads gradually.
- Information can become inaccurate over time.
- People react to what they believe.
- The player is not automatically told the truth.
- Multiple versions of the same story may exist simultaneously.

Following these principles allows the world to generate believable politics, conspiracies, mysteries, heroes, villains, and legends without requiring scripted outcomes.# Player Reputation

Player Reputation represents how the world perceives the player based on their actions, accomplishments, failures, and the information that has spread about them.

Unlike NPC Memory, Player Reputation is not tied to a single individual. Instead, it reflects the collective opinion of a group, region, kingdom, or faction.

The player's reputation is constantly evolving throughout the game.

---

## Reputation Is Contextual

The player does not have one universal reputation.

Instead, multiple reputations exist simultaneously.

For example:

- A hero in one kingdom.
- A wanted criminal in another.
- Unknown in a distant land.
- Respected by merchants.
- Feared by assassins.
- Distrusted by nobles.

Every major group forms its own opinion of the player.

---

## Reputation Categories

Player reputation may exist for many different groups.

Examples include:

### Local Reputation

How individual towns and cities view the player.

---

### Regional Reputation

How an entire region perceives the player.

---

### Kingdom Reputation

The player's standing with an entire nation.

---

### Faction Reputation

Relationships with guilds, armies, religious orders, criminal organizations, noble houses, and other factions.

---

### Social Reputation

How different social classes perceive the player.

Examples include:

- Common Folk
- Nobility
- Merchants
- Scholars
- Soldiers
- Criminals
- Adventurers

Each group may respond differently to the same actions.

---

## Reputation Levels

Instead of displaying numerical values, reputation is represented through descriptive titles.

Possible reputation levels include:

- Unknown
- Recognized
- Familiar
- Respected
- Admired
- Revered
- Legendary

Negative reputation may include:

- Distrusted
- Disliked
- Feared
- Hated
- Notorious
- Infamous

The AI determines which title best represents the player's current reputation.

Hidden numerical values may exist internally for balancing, but players primarily interact with descriptive rankings.

---

## Building Reputation

Reputation changes naturally through the player's actions.

Examples include:

- Completing quests
- Defeating powerful enemies
- Winning tournaments
- Helping civilians
- Saving settlements
- Discovering ancient ruins
- Becoming wealthy
- Starting businesses
- Committing crimes
- Breaking promises
- Showing mercy
- Executing prisoners
- Donating to charities
- Supporting political leaders

Not every action affects every reputation.

Helping one faction may anger another.

---

## Reputation Is Earned

Reputation should never increase simply because the player gains levels.

The world reacts to what the player has accomplished, not how powerful they are.

A Level 90 adventurer who avoids civilization may still be completely unknown.

Meanwhile, a Level 15 hero who saved an entire kingdom could already be legendary.

Actions matter more than statistics.

---

## Reputation Is Not Immediate

Reputation changes should not occur instantly.

News requires time to spread.

Factors affecting the speed of reputation growth include:

- Number of witnesses
- Importance of the event
- Distance
- Communication methods
- Political censorship
- Magical communication
- Bards and storytellers
- Merchant caravans

Major events spread much faster than ordinary acts.

---

## Player Knowledge

The player should not always know exactly how they are viewed.

Instead of displaying precise percentages or numerical values, the Reputation Journal provides a general overview of how different groups perceive them.

This encourages players to learn about their own reputation through conversations, exploration, and observation rather than constantly monitoring statistics.

---

## Design Principles

The Player Reputation system follows these rules:

- Reputation is earned through actions.
- Different groups maintain separate reputations.
- Reputation spreads through information.
- Reputation changes gradually over time.
- Reputation influences how the world reacts to the player.
- Reputation reflects perception, not absolute truth.# NPC Reputation

Player characters are not the only individuals who possess a reputation.

Every major NPC has a public reputation that influences how the world interacts with them.

An NPC's reputation represents what society believes about that character rather than who they truly are.

NPC reputations influence:

- Dialogue
- Trust
- Prices
- Political influence
- Leadership
- Recruitment
- Quest availability
- Public reactions
- Relationships with other NPCs

Different groups may view the same NPC differently.

Examples:

- A king may be loved by the nobility but hated by common folk.
- A pirate captain may be feared by merchants but admired by criminals.
- A priest may be respected publicly while secretly leading a cult.

NPC reputation changes throughout the game as events unfold.

Players may influence an NPC's reputation through their own actions.

---

# Faction Reputation

Every faction maintains its own opinion of the player.

Examples include:

- Kingdom Armies
- Merchant Guilds
- Religious Orders
- Criminal Organizations
- Noble Houses
- Adventurer Guilds
- Mercenary Companies
- Mage Academies

Helping one faction may improve relations while damaging another.

Faction reputation affects:

- Available quests
- Discounts
- Unique rewards
- Training opportunities
- Restricted areas
- Military support
- Political alliances

Faction reputation should always reflect the faction's goals and values.

---

# Regional Reputation

Different regions develop their own opinions of the player.

A famous hero in one province may be completely unknown in another.

Regional reputation spreads naturally through:

- Travelers
- Trade routes
- Local governments
- Inns
- Markets
- Rumors

Distance slows the spread of information.

---

# Kingdom Reputation

Entire nations develop opinions of the player.

Kingdom reputation reflects the player's overall relationship with a country's government and citizens.

Examples:

- Honored Defender
- Trusted Ally
- Wanted Criminal
- Enemy of the Crown
- National Hero

Kingdom reputation influences:

- Border crossings
- Guard behavior
- Political invitations
- Military assistance
- Royal audiences
- Diplomatic relations

---

# Fame and Infamy

Not all reputation is positive.

Fame represents positive recognition.

Infamy represents negative recognition.

Both make the player well known.

Examples of Fame:

- Dragonslayer
- Hero of Ashfall
- Savior of the Kingdom

Examples of Infamy:

- King's Bane
- Crimson Butcher
- The Black Raven

Both fame and infamy create opportunities as well as consequences.

---

# Hidden Reputation

Not every reputation should be visible.

Some groups may secretly admire or despise the player without revealing it immediately.

Examples include:

- Hidden cults
- Secret societies
- Underground resistance
- Assassin guilds
- Royal spies

Players often discover these reputations through dialogue and exploration rather than menus.

---

# Secret Identities

Reputation belongs to identities rather than individuals.

If the player hides their identity successfully, their actions may build the reputation of an alias instead.

Examples:

- The Crimson Raven
- The Silver Wolf
- The Hooded Knight

Few people may know the player's true identity.

This allows players to maintain multiple public identities throughout the game.

---

# Rumors and Witnesses

Information spreads through people.

Witnesses form the foundation of reputation.

Rumors evolve as they spread.

Stories may become:

- Exaggerated
- Simplified
- Distorted
- Completely false

Credible witnesses spread information more effectively than unreliable sources.

The AI should naturally generate rumors based on world events.

---

# Reputation Growth

Reputation develops gradually.

Factors affecting reputation growth include:

- Number of witnesses
- Importance of the event
- Existing reputation
- Distance
- Political influence
- Public interest

Small actions build reputation slowly.

Major historical events may transform reputation overnight.

---

# Reputation Decay

Reputation is not permanent.

Over time people forget.

New generations arise.

Current events replace old stories.

Positive and negative reputations naturally fade unless reinforced by future actions.

Legendary historical figures remain remembered much longer than ordinary adventurers.

---

# Reputation Effects

Reputation influences nearly every social interaction.

Possible effects include:

- Dialogue changes
- Different greetings
- Merchant prices
- Guard behavior
- Companion loyalty
- Recruitment
- Quest availability
- Political opportunities
- Marriage proposals
- Bounty hunters
- Crowds recognizing the player
- Children asking for stories
- Criminal organizations seeking alliances

Reputation should create opportunities rather than simply rewards or punishments.

---

# Reputation Journal

The journal should summarize:

- Local reputation
- Regional reputation
- Kingdom reputation
- Faction reputation
- Titles earned
- Public rumors
- Known enemies
- Allies

The journal only displays information the player has learned.

Unknown information remains hidden.

# Balance Philosophy

Reputation exists to make the world feel believable.

It should never function as a simple morality meter.

There are no universally "good" or "evil" actions.

Different cultures, kingdoms, and factions judge actions differently.

The same decision may improve one reputation while damaging another.

The player should never be able to satisfy everyone.

Meaningful choices require meaningful consequences.

# Earned Titles

As the player's reputation grows, they may earn titles that become part of their identity throughout the world.

Titles are awarded based on significant actions, achievements, or milestones rather than the player's level.

Different people may know the player by different titles depending on what information has reached them.

A player may possess multiple titles simultaneously.

Examples include:

- Dragonslayer
- Hero of Ashfall
- The Crimson Raven
- King's Bane
- Savior of Westfall
- Friend of the Forge King
- Champion of the Arena
- The Silent Blade

Titles are more than cosmetic.

NPCs may refer to the player using their earned titles during dialogue.

Some titles may inspire admiration, while others create fear, distrust, or respect.

Certain quests, dialogue options, and world events may become available only after earning specific titles.

Titles earned under a secret identity remain associated with that identity until the player's true identity becomes known.
