# 1. Quest Philosophy

## Design Goals

- Every quest should feel meaningful.
- Player choices should permanently shape the world.
- No two adventures should be exactly alike.
- Quests should encourage exploration, strategy, and roleplaying.
- The AI should generate quests that naturally fit the current world and story.

---

# 2. Quest Types

Quest categories include:

- Main Quests
- Side Quests
- Companion Quests
- Faction Quests
- World Events
- Dynamic Quests
- Hidden Quests
- Repeatable Quests

Each quest type serves a different purpose within the world.

---

# 3. Main Quests

Main quests progress the primary storyline.

Characteristics:

- Large scale
- Multiple stages
- Major decisions
- Permanent world consequences
- Multiple possible endings

---

# 4. Side Quests

Side quests provide optional stories.

They may:

- Introduce new characters.
- Unlock equipment.
- Reward experience.
- Expand the world's lore.
- Change future events.

Side quests should feel like complete stories rather than simple tasks.

---

# 5. Dynamic Quests

Dynamic quests are created by the AI based on the current state of the world.

Examples:

- A nearby village is attacked.
- A merchant goes missing.
- A kingdom declares war.
- A monster migrates into a forest.
- A companion asks for help.
- A famine causes unrest.

Dynamic quests make every playthrough unique.

---

# 6. Quest Objectives

Objectives may include:

- Investigation
- Exploration
- Combat
- Survival
- Diplomacy
- Escort
- Rescue
- Stealth
- Crafting
- Puzzle Solving
- Negotiation

Quests should combine multiple objective types whenever appropriate.

---

# 7. Player Choice

Players are free to approach quests however they choose.

Possible solutions may include:

- Combat
- Stealth
- Persuasion
- Bribery
- Diplomacy
- Deception
- Ignoring the quest entirely

The AI should react naturally to player decisions.

---

# 8. Quest Failure

Quests can fail.

Failure should create new stories instead of simply ending gameplay.

Examples:

- An NPC dies.
- A village is destroyed.
- A faction loses trust.
- A rival completes the objective first.
- The world changes permanently.

Failure is considered a valid outcome.

---

# 9. Quest Rewards

Possible rewards include:

- Experience
- Gold
- Equipment
- Ability Points
- Reputation
- Companion Loyalty
- New Quests
- Rare Materials
- Unique Items

Not every quest should reward combat-focused players equally.

---

# 10. AI Quest Generation

The AI generates quests using:

- Current world events
- NPC relationships
- Faction conflicts
- Player reputation
- Previous player choices
- World history
- Companion relationships
- Active world threats

Generated quests should always remain consistent with the established world.

---

# 11. World Consequences

Completed and failed quests permanently affect the world.

Possible consequences include:

- Cities growing or declining.
- NPCs remembering past actions.
- Kingdoms changing alliances.
- Prices increasing or decreasing.
- New locations becoming available.
- Existing locations being destroyed.
- Characters surviving or dying.

Player decisions should continue influencing future quests.

---

# 12. Quest Tracking

The journal records:

- Active quests
- Completed quests
- Failed quests
- Optional objectives
- Major choices
- Important characters
- Locations
- Rewards

Players may review previous decisions at any time.

---

# 13. Balance Philosophy

## Design Goals

- Every quest should tell a memorable story.
- Player freedom should always be respected.
- Multiple solutions should exist whenever possible.
- Consequences should feel believable.
- Exploration should be rewarded.
- The AI should avoid repetitive quest structures.
