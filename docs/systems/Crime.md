# Crime Philosophy

The crime system of **The Shattered Realms** is designed to create believable consequences for unlawful actions without restricting player freedom.

Crime should be treated as a living part of the world rather than a simple "wanted level."

Whether an action is considered illegal depends on the laws, customs, and governing authorities of the current campaign.

Players are always free to break the law, but doing so should naturally influence how individuals, organizations, and governments respond.

---

## Core Principles

### Laws Are Setting Dependent

Every campaign determines its own legal system.

An action that is illegal in one setting may be perfectly acceptable in another.

Examples include:

- Magic restrictions
- Weapon regulations
- Artificial intelligence laws
- Religious laws
- Smuggling
- Trespassing
- Theft
- Murder

The AI Game Master should generate laws that fit the world's culture, technology, and history.

---

### Crime Requires Knowledge

Authorities should only react to crimes they reasonably know about.

For a crime to produce legal consequences, there must generally be some combination of:

- Witnesses
- Evidence
- Reports
- Confessions
- Investigations

Players should not become wanted simply because the game knows they committed a crime.

---

### Meaningful Consequences

Crime should create believable consequences rather than instant punishment.

Possible consequences include:

- Arrest
- Investigation
- Bounties
- Reputation Loss
- Faction Hostility
- Increased Security
- Restricted Access
- Political Consequences

Different crimes should produce different responses.

---

### Player Freedom

Players are free to:

- Obey the law
- Break the law
- Become vigilantes
- Join criminal organizations
- Work as bounty hunters
- Act as spies
- Smuggle contraband
- Corrupt officials
- Expose corruption

Every path should produce unique gameplay opportunities.

---

### Justice Is Imperfect

Authorities may make mistakes.

Witnesses may lie.

Evidence may be incomplete.

Corrupt officials may abuse their power.

Innocent people may become suspects.

Guilty people may escape justice.

The legal system should feel believable rather than infallible.

---

### Crime Is Local

Crime is generally handled by the organizations responsible for maintaining order within a particular region.

These may include:

- City Guards
- Sheriffs
- Kingdom Armies
- Corporate Security
- Planetary Authorities
- Religious Orders
- Tribal Councils
- Private Security

Different regions may enforce different laws with different levels of severity.

---

### World Integration

The crime system interacts directly with:

- Reputation
- Factions
- NPCs
- Dialogue
- Economy
- Quests
- Kingdoms
- World Events
- Companions

Actions within the legal system may naturally influence these systems, and vice versa.

---

## Design Philosophy

Crime should create stories rather than simply punishing players.

The goal is to encourage meaningful choices by allowing the world to respond naturally to lawful and unlawful behavior.

# Witnesses

Witnesses play an important role in reporting criminal activity.

Witnesses may:

- Recognize the player
- Misidentify suspects
- Flee
- Report crimes
- Stay silent
- Lie
- Accept bribes
- Become intimidated

Whether a witness acts depends on factors such as:

- Courage
- Loyalty
- Reputation
- Relationships
- Personality
- Fear
- Distance from the crime

Not every witness reacts the same way.

---

# Evidence

Authorities rely on evidence rather than perfect knowledge.

Possible evidence includes:

- Stolen Property
- Fingerprints
- DNA
- Footprints
- Blood
- Surveillance Footage
- Magical Traces
- Weapon Residue
- Financial Records
- Digital Logs
- Witness Testimony

The types of evidence available should match the campaign setting.

Evidence may be:

- Hidden
- Destroyed
- Forged
- Planted
- Misinterpreted

Investigators should reach conclusions based only on available information.

---

# Suspicion

Suspicion represents how strongly authorities or individuals believe someone may have committed a crime.

Suspicion may increase through:

- Witness Reports
- Circumstantial Evidence
- Unusual Behavior
- Previous Criminal History
- Association with Criminals
- Possession of Stolen Goods

Suspicion alone should not automatically result in conviction.

Different organizations may require different standards before taking action.

---

# Investigations

Authorities may investigate crimes before making arrests.

Investigations may involve:

- Interviewing Witnesses
- Collecting Evidence
- Searching Crime Scenes
- Tracking Suspects
- Following Financial Records
- Reviewing Surveillance
- Consulting Experts
- Magical Investigation
- Digital Analysis

Complex investigations may continue over extended periods rather than ending immediately.

Player actions may influence the progress or outcome of an investigation.

---

# Law Enforcement

Every campaign determines who enforces the law.

Possible organizations include:

- City Guards
- Sheriffs
- Kingdom Armies
- Corporate Security
- Planetary Police
- Religious Orders
- Tribal Warriors
- Mercenary Companies
- Private Security

Law enforcement organizations differ in:

- Resources
- Competence
- Corruption
- Equipment
- Jurisdiction
- Authority

Some organizations may prioritize public safety.

Others may primarily protect those in power.

---

# Arrests

Authorities may attempt an arrest when they believe sufficient justification exists.

Possible responses available to the player include:

- Cooperate
- Flee
- Resist
- Negotiate
- Bribe
- Surrender Later
- Call Legal Assistance (if appropriate to the setting)

Not every arrest must result in combat.

Peaceful resolutions should remain viable whenever appropriate.

Different authorities may use different procedures depending on local laws and the severity of the alleged crime.

---

# Trials & Justice

Some campaign settings may include formal legal proceedings.

The structure of the justice system depends entirely on the world's laws and culture.

Possible forms of justice include:

- Jury Trials
- Judge Hearings
- Tribal Councils
- Religious Tribunals
- Military Courts
- Corporate Arbitration
- AI-Adjudicated Courts
- Summary Justice

Players may:

- Defend themselves
- Hire legal representation
- Present evidence
- Call witnesses
- Negotiate settlements
- Accept plea agreements (where appropriate)

Not every setting requires formal trials.

---

# Punishments

Punishments should reflect the severity of the crime and the values of the society enforcing the law.

Possible punishments include:

- Fines
- Community Service
- Imprisonment
- Confiscation of Property
- Temporary Exile
- Permanent Exile
- Loss of Rank or Titles
- Forced Labor
- Execution (where appropriate to the setting)

Punishments should have meaningful consequences without unnecessarily preventing continued gameplay.

---

# Bounties

Authorities or private organizations may place bounties on individuals.

Bounties may result from:

- Unpaid Crimes
- Escaping Arrest
- High-Profile Offenses
- Political Reasons
- Faction Conflicts

Higher bounties generally attract stronger and more experienced bounty hunters.

Bounties may be reduced, removed, or increased depending on player actions.

---

# Corruption

Officials may be:

- Honest
- Negligent
- Greedy
- Corrupt
- Intimidated
- Blackmailed

Corruption may influence:

- Investigations
- Arrests
- Trials
- Sentencing
- Evidence Handling

Players may choose to exploit or expose corruption depending on their goals.

---

# Criminal Organizations

Some factions operate outside the law.

Examples include:

- Smuggling Networks
- Organized Crime Families
- Pirate Fleets
- Hacker Collectives
- Assassin Guilds
- Black Market Syndicates
- Revolutionary Cells

Joining criminal organizations may unlock unique quests, merchants, contacts, and opportunities while creating conflict with lawful organizations.

---

# Escaping Justice

Players are not required to accept legal consequences immediately.

Possible methods of avoiding capture include:

- Hiding
- Disguises
- False Identities
- Destroying Evidence
- Bribery
- Smuggling
- Escaping Custody
- Negotiation
- Diplomatic Immunity (if appropriate)

Successfully avoiding capture should require planning and carry meaningful risks.

---

# Crime Reputation

Crime influences more than legal consequences.

Different groups may react differently to the same actions.

Examples include:

- Lawful authorities losing trust.
- Criminal organizations gaining respect.
- Civilians becoming fearful.
- Certain companions approving or disapproving.
- Factions changing their opinion.

Crime reputation should integrate naturally with the Reputation and Faction systems.

# Balance Philosophy

The crime system should reward creative problem-solving without encouraging random lawbreaking.

Breaking the law should create interesting stories, opportunities, and consequences rather than serving as a simple punishment system.

Players who choose lawful paths and players who choose criminal paths should both experience rewarding gameplay with different challenges and opportunities.

No single approach should always be the most profitable or enjoyable.
