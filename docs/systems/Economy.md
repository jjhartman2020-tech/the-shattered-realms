# Economy Philosophy

The economy of **The Shattered Realms** is designed to function as a living system rather than a collection of static shops and fixed prices.

Money flows throughout the world as kingdoms collect taxes, merchants trade goods, caravans transport supplies, craftsmen create equipment, factions purchase resources, and adventurers introduce valuable treasures into the market.

Markets should respond naturally to changes in the world while remaining understandable and enjoyable for players.

The player participates in the economy—they do not control it.

---

## Core Principles

### Living Economy

Economic activity continues whether or not the player is involved.

Merchants buy and sell goods.

Craftsmen produce equipment.

Caravans transport resources.

Kingdoms collect taxes.

Factions purchase supplies.

Cities consume food and materials.

The economy should feel active and believable at all times.

---

### Meaningful Wealth

Gold should remain valuable throughout the entire game.

Players should always have worthwhile ways to spend their wealth, including:

- Equipment
- Crafting Materials
- Housing
- Mounts
- Ship Travel
- Repairs
- Enchantments
- Rare Recipes
- Companion Equipment
- Kingdom Services
- Investments

Money should continue supporting player progression instead of becoming meaningless during the late game.

---

### Multiple Paths to Prosperity

Players should be able to earn wealth through many different playstyles.

Examples include:

- Quests
- Exploration
- Dungeon Loot
- Trading
- Crafting
- Gathering
- Hunting
- Bounties
- Faction Contracts
- Mercenary Work
- Treasure Hunting
- Selling Rare Discoveries

No single method should overwhelmingly outperform every other.

---

### Dynamic Markets

Prices and availability may change naturally based on world conditions.

Examples include:

- Wars increasing demand for weapons.
- Famines increasing food prices.
- Monster attacks disrupting trade.
- Closed trade routes reducing supplies.
- Festivals increasing demand for luxury goods.
- Natural disasters damaging local economies.

Changes should occur for believable reasons rather than random fluctuations.

---

### Risk and Reward

Greater profits should generally require greater risk.

Examples include:

- Trading across dangerous regions.
- Hunting rare monsters.
- Investing in expensive crafting projects.
- Recovering ancient treasures.
- Smuggling illegal goods.
- Escorting valuable caravans.

Players willing to accept greater danger should have opportunities for greater rewards.

---

### Fair Progression

The economy should reward intelligent decision-making without encouraging repetitive grinding.

Trading, crafting, combat, and exploration should all provide viable methods of earning wealth.

---

### World Integration

The economy interacts directly with:

- Inventory
- Equipment
- Crafting
- Quests
- Reputation
- Factions
- Kingdoms
- Crime
- Merchants
- NPCs
- Housing
- Exploration
- World Events

Economic changes should naturally influence other game systems, and those systems should likewise influence the economy.

---

## Design Philosophy

The economy should make the world feel alive while rewarding exploration, planning, and strategic decision-making.

Gold is more than a currency—it is one of the forces that drives the world's politics, trade, and daily life.

# Wealth & Value

Currency is only one measure of wealth.

Players may also accumulate wealth through:

- Property
- Valuable Equipment
- Rare Materials
- Collectibles
- Livestock
- Businesses
- Investments
- Ships
- Vehicles
- Land
- Artifacts

The value of an item depends on more than its rarity.

Factors that may influence value include:

- Supply
- Demand
- Quality
- Condition
- Location
- Craftsmanship
- Historical Importance
- Current World Events

The same item may have different values in different regions.

---

# Buying & Selling

Players may freely buy and sell goods with merchants willing to trade.

Transaction prices are influenced by factors such as:

- Reputation
- Merchant Personality
- Supply
- Demand
- Regional Prices
- Faction Relationships
- Negotiation
- Item Condition

Some merchants specialize in specific categories of goods and may refuse to purchase unrelated items.

Examples include:

- Weapon Dealers
- Armor Merchants
- Alchemists
- General Stores
- Jewelers
- Animal Traders
- Vehicle Dealers
- Black Market Traders

Buying and selling should feel like interacting with individuals rather than vending machines.

---

# Merchants

Merchants are independent NPCs with their own businesses, schedules, goals, and inventories.

Merchants may:

- Restock goods
- Travel between settlements
- Purchase supplies
- Hire guards
- Raise or lower prices
- Expand their businesses
- Lose inventory through theft or raids
- Close during emergencies
- Retire or be replaced

Merchant inventories are determined by:

- Profession
- Wealth
- Location
- Trade Routes
- Local Resources
- Current Events

Important merchants may become recurring characters rather than anonymous shopkeepers.

---

# Supply & Demand

The availability of goods influences their value.

Items that are scarce or difficult to obtain generally become more valuable.

Likewise, goods that are abundant may decrease in price.

Supply and demand may change due to:

- Harvests
- Wars
- Trade Disruptions
- Discoveries
- Monster Activity
- Population Growth
- Seasonal Changes
- Player Actions

The economy should respond gradually to these events instead of changing instantly.

---

# Regional Economies

Different regions naturally specialize in different industries.

Examples include:

- Mining Settlements
- Farming Communities
- Fishing Villages
- Industrial Cities
- Trade Ports
- Academic Centers
- Religious Capitals
- Military Strongholds

Regions generally produce goods that match their geography, culture, and available resources.

As a result, certain items may be inexpensive where they are produced but considerably more valuable elsewhere.

This encourages exploration, trade, and strategic planning.

---

# Trade Routes

Trade routes connect settlements and allow goods to move throughout the world.

Trade may occur by:

- Roads
- Rivers
- Oceans
- Air Routes
- Space Lanes
- Magical Portals
- Other setting-appropriate transportation methods

Trade routes influence:

- Merchant Inventories
- Regional Prices
- Resource Availability
- Settlement Prosperity

Trade routes may be disrupted by:

- Wars
- Bandits
- Pirates
- Monster Attacks
- Natural Disasters
- Political Conflict
- Severe Weather

Protecting or disrupting trade routes may become an important part of quests, faction conflicts, and world events.

---

# Taxes

Governments, organizations, or other ruling bodies may collect taxes depending on the campaign setting.

Taxes should always fit the world's lore and political structure.

Possible taxes include:

- Property Taxes
- Trade Taxes
- Import & Export Duties
- Guild Fees
- Docking Fees
- Road Tolls
- Business Licenses
- Resource Royalties

Taxes may fund:

- Military forces
- Infrastructure
- Public services
- Research
- Government operations

Tax systems should create roleplaying opportunities rather than unnecessary frustration.

---

# Banking

Some settings may include banking institutions.

Banks may offer services such as:

- Currency Storage
- Secure Deposits
- Currency Exchange
- Wealth Transfers
- Investment Opportunities
- Business Financing

Not every campaign requires banks.

Primitive settings may rely entirely on physical currency, while advanced settings may use digital or magical banking systems.

---

# Loans & Debt

Financial institutions, merchants, factions, or private individuals may offer loans.

Loans may be used to purchase:

- Property
- Businesses
- Vehicles
- Ships
- Expensive Equipment
- Crafting Projects

Failure to repay debts may result in consequences such as:

- Interest Charges
- Loss of Property
- Damaged Reputation
- Legal Action
- Bounty Hunters
- Faction Intervention

Debt should create meaningful stories rather than becoming an unavoidable punishment.

---

# Black Markets

Illegal or restricted goods may be traded through underground markets.

Examples include:

- Stolen Goods
- Contraband
- Forged Documents
- Illegal Weapons
- Experimental Technology
- Forbidden Artifacts
- Rare Creatures

Access may require:

- Criminal Reputation
- Secret Contacts
- Hidden Locations
- Special Quests
- Faction Membership

Using black markets may influence crime, reputation, and faction relationships.

---

# Loot Economy

Items found through exploration should naturally enter the economy.

Players may choose to:

- Equip them
- Sell them
- Salvage them
- Donate them
- Display them
- Trade them

Important or unique items should retain their significance and not become ordinary vendor trash.

Loot generation should remain balanced so that exploration feels rewarding without flooding the economy.

---

# Crafting Economy

Crafting should integrate naturally with the economy.

Crafted goods may be:

- Sold
- Traded
- Commissioned
- Donated
- Used personally

The profitability of crafting should depend on:

- Material Costs
- Market Demand
- Crafting Skill
- Competition
- Item Quality

Crafting should reward expertise without becoming the only profitable way to earn wealth.

---

# Faction Economies

Many factions maintain independent economies.

Examples include:

- Merchant Guilds
- Military Orders
- Religious Organizations
- Criminal Syndicates
- Noble Houses

Factions may:

- Purchase supplies
- Reward members
- Control trade
- Finance expeditions
- Invest in settlements
- Fund wars

Faction wealth may influence their political and military power.

---

# Kingdom Economies

Nations, governments, or other large political powers each maintain their own economies.

Economic strength may depend on:

- Population
- Natural Resources
- Agriculture
- Industry
- Trade
- Stability
- Technology
- Infrastructure

Economic prosperity may influence diplomacy, military strength, public happiness, and future world events.

---

# Inflation & Market Stability

The economy should remain balanced throughout the game.

Prices may gradually change due to:

- Resource shortages
- Major discoveries
- World Events
- Increased production
- Population changes

Economic changes should occur gradually and for believable reasons.

The AI Game Master should prevent excessive inflation or market collapse unless such events are intentional parts of the campaign.

---

# Dynamic Economy

The economy evolves alongside the world.

Economic conditions may change due to:

- Wars
- Peace Treaties
- Natural Disasters
- Festivals
- New Trade Routes
- Faction Conflicts
- Technological Advances
- Discoveries
- Political Changes
- Player Actions

No settlement should remain economically identical forever.

The world should continuously adapt to changing circumstances.

# Balance Philosophy

The economy should support every major playstyle without forcing players into repetitive grinding.

Trading, crafting, exploration, quests, combat, diplomacy, and entrepreneurship should all provide viable methods of earning wealth.

The strongest rewards should come from intelligent decision-making, calculated risk, and meaningful participation in the world.

Economic systems should enhance immersion without requiring players to become economists.

---

# Future Systems

The economic framework is designed to expand without requiring major redesign.

Possible future additions include:

- Player-Owned Businesses
- Stock Markets
- Corporations
- Shipping Companies
- Advanced Trade Networks
- Dynamic Investments
- Auctions
- Commodity Exchanges
- Insurance Systems
- Player-Run Marketplaces
- Economic Crises
- International Trade Agreements

Future systems should build upon the existing economy while preserving the philosophy of a living, believable world that can support campaigns in any setting.
