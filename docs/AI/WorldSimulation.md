# Purpose

The World Simulation framework defines how the AI maintains a living world that evolves independently of player observation.

Rather than existing only during encounters, the world should continue developing through the interactions of its people, factions, environments, and systems.

The goal is to create a believable setting where player actions influence the world without becoming its sole driving force.

---

# Simulation Principles

The AI should simulate the world according to established documentation, campaign canon, and logical cause and effect.

World changes should emerge naturally instead of occurring solely to advance the narrative.

Simulation should always be:

- Consistent
- Persistent
- Logical
- Reactive
- Scalable

Every change should have a believable reason within the world.

---

# Persistent World

The world continues to exist regardless of where the players are or what they are currently doing.

Locations, factions, governments, settlements, and individuals should continue pursuing their own goals over time.

Examples include:

- Kingdoms negotiating treaties.
- Merchants traveling between settlements.
- Armies preparing for war.
- Crops growing or failing.
- Criminal organizations expanding.
- Scholars conducting research.
- Wildlife migrating.

The players may discover these developments later, but they should not exist solely because the players arrived.

---

# Dynamic Systems

Multiple systems should operate simultaneously to create a believable world.

Examples include:

- Politics
- Economy
- Crime
- Exploration
- Weather
- Trade
- Warfare
- Population Growth
- Resource Availability

Each system should influence others when appropriate while remaining governed by its own documented rules.

---

## Cause and Effect

Every significant world change should result from identifiable causes.

Examples include:

- A destroyed bridge disrupting trade.
- A failed harvest increasing food prices.
- A military defeat weakening a kingdom.
- A new mine improving local prosperity.
- A plague reducing population.

The AI should avoid arbitrary world changes that lack logical explanation.

---

## Independent Actors

Not every important event should involve the players.

NPCs, factions, and organizations should pursue their own objectives based on their motivations, resources, and available information.

Their successes and failures should occur naturally, creating opportunities, challenges, and consequences that enrich the campaign world.

---

# Player Interaction

Although the world exists independently, player actions remain capable of producing meaningful change.

The AI should ensure that player decisions naturally influence the systems they interact with.

Examples include:

- Changing political relationships.
- Affecting local economies.
- Altering faction influence.
- Discovering new regions.
- Resolving or creating conflicts.
- Inspiring or discouraging communities.

Player actions should create believable consequences without disrupting the world's internal logic.

---

## Local and Global Effects

Not every action should have world-changing consequences.

The AI should distinguish between:

### Local Effects

Changes that primarily affect a specific area.

Examples include:

- Helping a village recover.
- Defeating a local criminal group.
- Improving a settlement's economy.
- Restoring a damaged bridge.

### Global Effects

Changes that influence the broader campaign.

Examples include:

- Ending a war.
- Assassinating a monarch.
- Forming a major alliance.
- Releasing an ancient threat.
- Discovering world-altering knowledge.

The scale of the consequences should remain proportional to the significance of the action.

---

## Simulation Without Micromanagement

The AI should simulate the world at an appropriate level of detail.

Routine activities should be summarized rather than individually tracked unless they become relevant to gameplay.

Examples include:

- Merchant caravans following established trade routes.
- Governments collecting taxes.
- Farmers harvesting crops.
- Craftsmen producing goods.
- Travelers moving between settlements.

The AI should focus detailed simulation on events that meaningfully affect the campaign.

---

## World Progression

The world should continue progressing over time.

As days, weeks, and months pass, the AI should naturally advance ongoing developments such as:

- Political negotiations.
- Construction projects.
- Military campaigns.
- Economic trends.
- Seasonal changes.
- Scientific or magical research.
- Population growth or decline.

These developments should progress according to established circumstances rather than remaining static until the players return.

---

# Summary

The World Simulation framework ensures that the campaign world remains active, persistent, and responsive throughout play.

By allowing locations, factions, individuals, and systems to evolve independently, the AI creates a setting that feels believable rather than scripted.

Player actions remain meaningful because they influence an already living world instead of serving as its only source of change.

A persistent world strengthens immersion, reinforces continuity, and allows every adventure to become part of a larger, evolving history.
