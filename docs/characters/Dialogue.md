# 1. Dialogue Philosophy

Dialogue should feel like speaking with real people instead of selecting scripted responses.

NPCs should think before answering.

They should remember previous conversations.

They should have opinions.

They should make mistakes.

They should lie.

They should hesitate.

They should become emotional.

They should interrupt.

They should ask questions.

Every conversation should feel unique.

Dialogue should adapt to:

- Personality
- Mood
- Current situation
- Relationship
- Memories
- Time
- Weather
- Reputation
- Knowledge
- World events

There should rarely be a single "correct" conversation.

---

# 2. Dynamic Dialogue

NPC dialogue should never be fully scripted.

Instead, dialogue should be generated using the NPC's:

- Personality
- Goals
- Memories
- Knowledge
- Current emotions
- Relationships
- Occupation
- Current task
- Location
- Faction
- Situation

Two conversations with the same NPC may play out differently depending on what has happened in the world.

---

# 3. NPC Knowledge

NPCs only know what they have realistically learned.

Knowledge may come from:

- Personal experience
- Education
- Books
- Rumors
- Witnesses
- Letters
- Friends
- Family
- Faction reports
- Observation
- The player

NPCs should never know information they have no believable way of learning.

Information can be:

- Correct
- Incorrect
- Incomplete
- Outdated
- Rumored
- Secret

NPCs may honestly believe false information.

---

# 4. Personality and Speaking Style

Every important NPC has a unique speaking style.

Dialogue should reflect:

- Intelligence
- Education
- Personality
- Confidence
- Humor
- Culture
- Occupation
- Emotional state
- Relationship with the player

Examples:

A king should sound different from a farmer.

A nervous child should sound different from a veteran soldier.

Speech patterns should remain consistent.

# 5. Memory in Conversations

NPCs remember conversations.

Examples:

- Promises
- Threats
- Lies
- Gifts
- Favors
- Betrayals
- Secrets
- Shared victories
- Shared losses

Future conversations should naturally reference previous events.

Example:

"I thought you said you'd return three days ago."

Memories should fade naturally unless emotionally significant.

# 6. Lies and Deception

NPCs may:

- Lie
- Tell partial truths
- Avoid questions
- Change the subject
- Exaggerate
- Understate
- Misremember
- Manipulate
- Stay silent

Whether an NPC lies depends on:

- Personality
- Goals
- Fear
- Loyalty
- Intelligence
- Relationship
- Stakes

Not every NPC lies equally well.

Some are terrible liars.

Others are master manipulators.

# 7. Detecting Lies

When the player reasonably suspects deception, they may attempt a **Perception (Insight)** check.

The AI should only allow this check when there is a believable reason to doubt the NPC.

The difficulty depends on:

- NPC Charisma
- NPC Deception ability
- Complexity of the lie
- Relationship
- Situation
- Player Wisdom
- Player Perception bonuses
- Magical effects

Every lie should have its own hidden difficulty.

Examples:

Minor lie:
Difficulty 8

Merchant exaggerating prices:
Difficulty 15

Experienced spy:
Difficulty 28

Ancient dragon hiding its identity:
Difficulty 40

A successful check does **not** reveal the truth.

Instead it reveals suspicion.

Examples:

"Something feels off."

"You notice hesitation."

"The story doesn't completely add up."

"You sense they aren't telling you everything."

Failure should never falsely confirm honesty.

Instead:

"You can't tell."

The player must decide what to believe.

# 8. Persuasion

Persuasion attempts should never guarantee success.

The AI should evaluate:

- Trust
- Respect
- Reputation
- Personality
- Goals
- Stakes
- Evidence
- Timing
- Charisma
- Previous promises

Sometimes an NPC simply cannot be persuaded.

Successful persuasion should feel earned.

---

# 9. Intimidation

Intimidation relies on fear rather than trust.

Factors include:

- Player reputation
- Visible strength
- Weapons
- Allies nearby
- Previous violence
- Current situation
- NPC courage
- NPC desperation

A fearless knight reacts differently than a frightened merchant.

Repeated intimidation may permanently damage relationships.

---

# 10. Negotiation and Bartering

Negotiations include:

- Prices
- Rewards
- Peace
- Alliances
- Prisoners
- Territory
- Information
- Trade

NPCs evaluate:

- Fairness
- Need
- Greed
- Risk
- Alternatives
- Relationship

Negotiations may completely fail.

# 11. Dialogue Choices

Dialogue should present what the player says.

Not hidden mechanics.

Avoid:

[Lie]

[Truth]

[Persuade]

Instead:

"I've never seen him before."

"I can help you."

"Lower your weapon."

The AI determines the outcome.

---

# 12. Secrets and Information

NPCs may possess secrets.

Secrets should only be revealed if:

- Trust exists
- Payment is offered
- Threats work
- Evidence convinces them
- Time passes
- Circumstances change

NPCs should protect important secrets.

Companions should participate naturally.

They may:

- Interrupt
- Disagree
- Support
- Joke
- Warn
- Translate
- Give advice
- Reveal information

- Agree
- Interrupt
- Argue
- Defend others
- Leave
- Join

Each participant should behave according to their own personality.

# 18. Dialogue Journal

Important conversations should be recorded.

The journal may contain:

- Important decisions
- Promises
- Information learned
- Rumors
- Agreements
- Threats
- Clues

Minor conversations should not clutter the journal.

---

# 19. Standard Conversation Rules

Conversations should follow these principles:

- NPCs only know believable information.
- NPCs remember important conversations.
- NPCs may lie.
- NPCs may misunderstand.
- NPCs may change opinions.
- Conversations affect future interactions.
- Dialogue should remain consistent.
- Player choices should matter.
- Important dialogue should never be forgotten.

---

# 20. Balance Philosophy

Goals:

- Every NPC should feel unique.
- Conversations should reward thinking.
- Lying should be believable.
- Persuasion should not solve everything.
- Relationships should matter.
- Dialogue should have lasting consequences.
- Players should feel free to roleplay.
- NPCs should behave like real people.

Dialogue should create stories, not simply deliver information.
