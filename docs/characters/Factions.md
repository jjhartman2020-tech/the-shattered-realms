# Faction Philosophy

Factions are living organizations that shape the world of **The Shattered Realms**.

Rather than existing solely to give quests or merchants, factions have their own goals, leadership, resources, allies, enemies, and influence. They operate independently of the player and continue pursuing their objectives whether the player becomes involved or not.

Joining a faction should be a meaningful decision. Membership provides opportunities, responsibilities, rewards, and consequences. Likewise, choosing to oppose a faction should have lasting effects on how that organization—and those connected to it—view the player.

The faction system is designed to create a dynamic world where political power, loyalty, reputation, and conflict evolve naturally over time.

---

## Core Principles

### Living Organizations

Every faction exists independently of the player.

Factions recruit members, complete objectives, defend territory, wage war, negotiate alliances, lose influence, gain power, and react to events occurring throughout the world.

The player is never the sole reason a faction exists.

---

### Meaningful Choices

Joining, helping, betraying, or attacking a faction should always have meaningful consequences.

Every decision should influence future opportunities without permanently locking players out of enjoying the game.

Different playthroughs should naturally produce different relationships with the world's factions.

---

### Reputation Matters

Each faction maintains its own opinion of the player.

Helping one faction may improve its opinion while damaging relationships with rival organizations.

Faction reputation is separate from the world's general Reputation system but interacts with it when appropriate.

---

### Dynamic Relationships

Factions are constantly interacting with one another.

Relationships may include:

- Allies
- Rivals
- Neutral Parties
- Trading Partners
- Political Rivals
- Military Enemies

These relationships may change as the world evolves.

---

### Internal Structure

Every faction has its own hierarchy, leadership, traditions, rules, and culture.

Different factions should feel unique rather than functioning as simple quest hubs.

---

### Player Freedom

Players are free to:

- Join factions
- Leave factions
- Betray factions
- Ignore factions
- Destroy certain factions
- Create enemies
- Earn trust
- Rise through the ranks

The world should react appropriately to every major decision.

---

### Consequences Without Punishment

Faction choices should create new opportunities rather than simply removing content.

Choosing one path may close certain doors while opening entirely different ones.

The goal is replayability, not punishment.

---

### World Integration

The faction system is deeply connected with other game systems, including:

- Reputation
- Quests
- NPC Relationships
- Dialogue
- Crime
- Economy
- Kingdom Politics
- Warfare
- Companions
- Exploration

Actions taken within one system may naturally influence faction relationships throughout the world.

---

## Design Philosophy

Factions should feel like real organizations with their own ambitions, conflicts, and identities.

The player participates in the world's politics—they do not control them.
