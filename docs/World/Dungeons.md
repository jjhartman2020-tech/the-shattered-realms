# Purpose

The Dungeons framework defines structured adventure locations that provide concentrated opportunities for exploration, problem-solving, combat, and storytelling.

A dungeon is not defined by its appearance, but by its role within the campaign.

Whether it is a cave, fortress, temple, forest, shipwreck, palace, or any other environment, a dungeon should present a cohesive adventure with meaningful objectives and consequences.

---

# Dungeon Principles

Every dungeon should strive to be:

- Purposeful
- Believable
- Rewarding
- Context-Aware
- Replayable

Each dungeon should feel like a natural part of the world rather than an isolated gameplay challenge.

Its existence should have a logical explanation based on the surrounding history, geography, politics, or world events.

---

# Dungeon Structure

While every dungeon is unique, most should contain a combination of:

- Exploration
- Combat
- Puzzles
- Environmental Hazards
- Roleplaying Opportunities
- Secrets
- Meaningful Decisions

Not every dungeon requires every element, but each should encourage players to engage with more than combat alone.

Players should have multiple ways to progress whenever appropriate.

---

## Dungeon Objectives

A dungeon should provide a clear purpose beyond simply reaching the end.

Objectives may include:

- Recovering an artifact.
- Rescuing prisoners.
- Eliminating a threat.
- Investigating mysterious events.
- Escaping confinement.
- Negotiating with inhabitants.
- Exploring forgotten history.
- Activating or disabling ancient mechanisms.

Objectives should encourage meaningful interaction with the environment.

---

# Challenges

Challenges should arise naturally from the dungeon's theme and purpose.

Examples include:

- Hostile creatures.
- Traps.
- Collapsing structures.
- Environmental dangers.
- Magical anomalies.
- Locked passages.
- Resource scarcity.
- Moral dilemmas.
- Time-sensitive objectives.

Challenges should encourage observation, preparation, teamwork, and creativity rather than relying exclusively on combat.

---

# Progression

Players should experience a sense of progression while exploring a dungeon.

Progress may occur through:

- Unlocking new areas.
- Solving puzzles.
- Discovering shortcuts.
- Acquiring keys or information.
- Overcoming major encounters.
- Revealing hidden sections.

Progression should reward exploration and logical thinking while minimizing unnecessary backtracking.

---

# Rewards

Dungeon rewards should reflect the nature of the adventure.

Rewards may include:

- Equipment
- Resources
- Knowledge
- Story Progression
- Reputation
- New Allies
- New Locations
- Crafting Materials
- Unique Abilities
- Rare Collectibles

The most valuable reward is not always treasure.

Information, relationships, or access to future opportunities may have greater long-term value.

---

# Replayability

Completed dungeons should remain part of the world.

Depending on the campaign, a dungeon may:

- Become abandoned.
- Be repopulated.
- Change ownership.
- Reveal newly accessible areas.
- Become a settlement.
- Serve as a future quest location.

The AI should treat completed dungeons as evolving locations rather than permanently finished content.

---

# Interaction With Other Systems

The Dungeons framework governs structured adventure locations while relying on other documented systems for specific mechanics.

Examples include:

- **Exploration** governs discovering dungeons.
- **Travel** governs reaching them.
- **Combat** governs battles within them.
- **Quests** provide objectives.
- **Equipment** and **Inventory** support preparation.
- **World Simulation** allows dungeons to change over time.
- **Procedural Generation** creates new dungeon layouts, encounters, and objectives when appropriate.

This framework defines what a dungeon is without redefining the mechanics managed elsewhere.

---

# Summary

The Dungeons framework defines adventure locations that combine exploration, challenge, and meaningful decision-making within a cohesive environment.

Rather than serving as isolated collections of encounters, dungeons should reflect the world's history and evolve alongside the campaign.

Every dungeon should tell a story through its layout, inhabitants, objectives, and consequences, ensuring that completing it changes both the players and the world around them.
