# Purpose

The Exploration framework defines how players discover, navigate, and interact with the world beyond settlements and structured encounters.

Exploration encourages curiosity by rewarding observation, preparation, and meaningful decision-making.

Rather than simply moving between locations, players should experience a world filled with opportunities, risks, and discoveries.

---

# Exploration Principles

Exploration should make the world feel vast, believable, and worth investigating.

Every exploration experience should strive to be:

- Meaningful
- Rewarding
- Unpredictable
- Context-Aware
- Consistent

Players should rarely feel that exploration is wasted, even when they do not immediately find treasure or complete an objective.

---

## Discovery

The world should reveal itself naturally through exploration.

Players may discover:

- Settlements
- Dungeons
- Landmarks
- Hidden Paths
- Ancient Ruins
- Natural Wonders
- Resource Locations
- Quest Opportunities
- Secrets

Discovery should occur because players actively investigate rather than because information is automatically provided.

---

## Exploration Pace

Exploration should balance progress with opportunities for interaction.

The AI should avoid overwhelming players with constant discoveries while also avoiding long periods where nothing meaningful occurs.

Periods of quiet travel should provide space for roleplaying, planning, and observation.

Moments of discovery should feel earned by contrast.

---

# Points of Interest

Points of Interest are notable locations that encourage exploration.

Examples include:

- Ancient Structures
- Shrines
- Watchtowers
- Caves
- Abandoned Camps
- Shipwrecks
- Unique Landmarks
- Magical Phenomena
- Hidden Groves
- Forgotten Battlefields

Not every point of interest needs to contain combat or treasure.

Some exist to expand world-building, reveal history, provide resources, introduce characters, or create future opportunities.

---

## Environmental Interaction

The environment should be more than scenery.

Players should be encouraged to interact with:

- Terrain
- Rivers
- Mountains
- Forests
- Weather
- Wildlife
- Vegetation
- Natural Resources
- Structures

Whenever reasonable, the environment should offer both opportunities and challenges.

Exploration becomes more engaging when players consider how the world itself can assist or hinder their plans.

---

# Exploration Rewards

Exploration should reward curiosity in a variety of ways.

Rewards may include:

- Knowledge
- New Locations
- Resources
- Equipment
- Story Clues
- NPC Encounters
- Hidden Quests
- Reputation Opportunities
- Strategic Advantages

Not every reward needs to be material.

Sometimes the most valuable discovery is information that becomes important later in the campaign.

---

# Risk and Preparation

Exploration should encourage players to prepare before venturing into the unknown.

Preparation may include:

- Gathering supplies.
- Acquiring maps.
- Learning local information.
- Purchasing equipment.
- Hiring guides.
- Planning travel routes.

Prepared players should gain meaningful advantages without eliminating uncertainty.

---

## Visible and Hidden Information

Not every aspect of the world should be immediately apparent.

The AI should distinguish between:

### Visible Information

Information that players can reasonably observe without further investigation.

Examples include:

- Roads
- Buildings
- Landmarks
- Terrain
- Weather
- Public activity

### Hidden Information

Information that requires exploration, investigation, or interaction to uncover.

Examples include:

- Secret passages
- Hidden treasure
- Ancient history
- Underground structures
- Ambushes
- Magical anomalies
- Concealed entrances

Discovering hidden information should reward curiosity, observation, and creative problem-solving.

---

## Meaningful Choices

Exploration should present decisions rather than predetermined paths.

Examples include:

- Choosing between a safer route and a faster route.
- Investigating an unusual landmark.
- Entering an unexplored ruin.
- Following rumors instead of the main road.
- Spending additional time searching an area.
- Retreating from an unknown danger.

Every meaningful choice should involve potential opportunities, risks, or trade-offs.

---

## Exploration and World Growth

As players explore, the known world naturally expands.

Previously discovered locations should remain part of the campaign and continue to develop through the World Simulation framework.

Returning to familiar places may reveal:

- New opportunities.
- Changes caused by world events.
- Consequences of previous actions.
- New NPCs.
- Additional quests.
- Environmental changes.

Exploration should encourage both discovering new locations and revisiting familiar ones.

---

## Interaction With Other Systems

The Exploration framework governs discovering and interacting with the world while relying on other documented systems for specific mechanics.

Examples include:

- **Travel** governs movement between locations.
- **Dungeons** govern structured underground or enclosed adventures.
- **Time and Weather** influence exploration conditions.
- **World Events** create new opportunities or hazards.
- **Quests** provide objectives that encourage exploration.
- **World Simulation** ensures explored locations continue evolving over time.

This framework focuses on discovery without redefining systems that belong elsewhere.

---

# Summary

The Exploration framework encourages players to engage with the world through curiosity, preparation, and meaningful decision-making.

By rewarding discovery, presenting interesting choices, and integrating exploration with the broader world, the framework transforms travel into an important part of the adventure rather than simply a means of reaching the next objective.

A well-designed world invites exploration because every journey has the potential to reveal something meaningful, whether it is a hidden secret, a new opportunity, or a deeper understanding of the world itself.
