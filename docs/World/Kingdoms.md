# Purpose

The Kingdoms framework defines how sovereign political entities operate within the world.

While campaigns may feature kingdoms, empires, republics, city-states, tribal confederations, or other forms of government, they all follow the principles established in this document.

This framework governs nations themselves rather than the factions, organizations, or individuals that exist within them.

---

# Political Entities

A political entity is any organized society that exercises authority over a defined territory.

Examples include:

- Kingdoms
- Empires
- Republics
- City-States
- Tribal Confederations
- Theocracies
- Merchant Leagues
- Custom Governments

Each political entity should possess its own identity, leadership, culture, and interests.

---

## Government

Every political entity should have a governing structure that determines how authority is exercised.

Examples include:

- Monarchies
- Councils
- Democracies
- Religious Leadership
- Military Rule
- Noble Houses
- Custom Systems

The chosen form of government should influence how decisions are made without restricting campaign creativity.

---

## Leadership

Political leaders are responsible for directing the nation according to its governing system.

Leadership may consist of:

- A Monarch
- A President
- A Council
- A High Priest
- A Military Commander
- Multiple Governing Bodies

Leadership should influence national policy, diplomacy, law, and military decisions according to the established government.

---

# Territory

Political entities control defined regions of the world.

Territory may include:

- Capitals
- Cities
- Towns
- Villages
- Castles
- Fortresses
- Roads
- Farmland
- Wilderness
- Strategic Resources

Territorial control determines where a nation's laws, military, and governing authority apply.

Border regions may be disputed, shared, or actively contested depending on current world events.

---

# Culture

Every political entity should develop its own cultural identity.

Culture may include:

- Traditions
- Holidays
- Languages
- Architecture
- Religion
- Social Customs
- Values
- Historical Influences

Culture should shape daily life and distinguish one nation from another without making every settlement identical.

---

# Diplomacy

Political entities rarely exist in isolation.

Each nation should maintain relationships with neighboring political entities based upon its history, interests, resources, ideology, and current circumstances.

Diplomatic relationships may include:

- Alliances
- Neutral Relations
- Trade Agreements
- Military Pacts
- Rivalries
- Territorial Disputes
- Open Conflict

Diplomacy should evolve naturally through world events and player actions.

---

## Laws and Authority

Political entities establish and enforce laws within their territory.

Laws may govern areas such as:

- Property
- Crime
- Commerce
- Magic
- Travel
- Military Service
- Citizenship

The application of laws should remain consistent with the nation's government, culture, and values.

Law enforcement belongs to the governing authority of the political entity rather than individual factions unless specifically established by the campaign.

---

## Military

Most political entities maintain some form of organized defense.

Military organizations may include:

- Standing Armies
- City Guards
- Royal Knights
- Naval Forces
- Border Patrols
- Local Militias

The size, organization, and effectiveness of a military should reflect the nation's resources, population, and political priorities.

Military forces primarily defend the interests of the political entity rather than acting as independent organizations.

---

## National Development

Political entities should evolve throughout a campaign.

Examples include:

- Leadership changes.
- Economic growth or decline.
- Territorial expansion or loss.
- Population changes.
- Technological or magical advancement.
- Internal reforms.
- Diplomatic shifts.

These developments should emerge naturally through the World Simulation framework.

---

## Interaction With Other Systems

The Kingdoms framework provides the political foundation for the world while working alongside other documented systems.

Examples include:

- **Settlements** define the communities located within political entities.
- **Factions** define organizations that may operate inside or across multiple political entities.
- **Reputation** influences how political authorities respond to the players.
- **Crime** determines legal consequences within each nation's jurisdiction.
- **World Events** may reshape political entities over time.

This framework establishes where political authority exists without redefining the systems governed elsewhere.

---

# Summary

The Kingdoms framework defines how sovereign political entities govern territory, establish laws, maintain diplomacy, and develop over time.

Rather than prescribing a single style of government, it provides a flexible foundation capable of supporting kingdoms, empires, republics, city-states, tribal confederations, and other forms of political organization.

By separating political entities from factions, the framework ensures that governments, organizations, and world systems each maintain clear and distinct responsibilities while contributing to a cohesive and believable world.
